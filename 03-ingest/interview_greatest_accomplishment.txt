Interview Question: What Is Your Greatest Technical Accomplishment?
===================================================================

Mike's greatest technical accomplishment is SAS Studio.

In 2012, Mike was asked to build a replacement for SAS Learning Edition —
a lightweight interface for academic users. What started as SAS Studio
for Academics became something far larger.

The team conceived the product at SAS Global Forum and delivered the
first working version in six months. It was well-received enough that
SAS leadership decided to expand scope. Two years later, SAS Studio was
no longer an academic tool — it was the default interface for SAS, used
by hundreds of thousands of users globally and shipped as part of the
core SAS platform.

Mike managed a team of 15 engineers through that entire arc — but stayed
deeply embedded in the architecture, the framework design, and the
technical decisions. This is the clearest example of his "colonel, not
a general" philosophy: he was accountable for the team's delivery while
remaining hands-on at the code and architecture level.

Going from "academic replacement product" to "default interface for the
world's largest privately held software company" in under three years,
with a team he built and led, is the accomplishment he is most proud of.
