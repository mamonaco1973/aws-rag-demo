Interview Question: What If You Haven't Done Something Before?
=============================================================

When asked about a technology or skill he has not used before, Mike's
honest answer is: "I haven't done that yet, but how hard could it be?"

That is not overconfidence. It is a philosophy grounded in a consistent
track record of picking up unfamiliar technology, building something real
with it, and shipping it. The YouTube channel is the proof — every video
represents a technology Mike decided to learn by doing. Multi-cloud
Kubernetes, RStudio clusters with shared NFS storage and managed
directories, serverless MCP services, AI image transformation pipelines,
queue-driven async workers across four cloud providers. None of these
were already in his back pocket when he started.

The approach is always the same:
- Read the documentation and understand the design intent
- Find the constraints and sharp edges before they find you
- Build a working reference implementation — not a tutorial copy, a
  real architecture that would survive production
- Verify it, document it, explain it publicly

Mike does not treat "I haven't done this" as a ceiling. It is just the
starting point. The question he actually cares about is: what does good
look like for this, and how quickly can he get there?

In consulting engagements this mindset matters practically. Clients do
not always give you six months to get comfortable with their stack
before expectations kick in. Mike's value is the ability to come up to
speed fast, ask the right questions early, and deliver something solid
without requiring a long runway.

The attitude is not "it will be easy." Sometimes it is genuinely hard.
The point is that hard has never been a reason to stop.
