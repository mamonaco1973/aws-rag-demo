Interview Question: Tell Me About a Time You Disagreed With a Technical Direction
================================================================================

Mike has worked on both sides of the product vs. consulting divide —
18 years building products at SAS Institute, followed by several years
in pure consulting at d-wise and Instem. That dual background gives him
a clear view of where companies go wrong when they try to do both
simultaneously.

In consulting, a degree of confidence ahead of certainty is normal and
healthy. A consultant commits to delivering something that exists in the
technology landscape and figures out the specific implementation during
the engagement. That is not dishonesty — the technology is real, the
consultant is capable, and delivery follows.

The problem comes when that same "fake it till you make it" mindset gets
applied to product development. Shipping a product requires fundamentally
different discipline — roadmaps, repeatability, QA, versioning, customer
support at scale. "We will figure it out" works for a one-time consulting
engagement. It does not work when you are promising a feature to a
hundred customers on a release date.

Mike has seen companies attempt this transition without changing how they
think about commitments and delivery. The instinct that made them great
consultants becomes a liability in product development. The symptoms look
like execution problems — missed dates, quality issues — but the root
cause is cultural. Having built real products at SAS and then spent years
in consulting, he can recognize that mismatch quickly.
