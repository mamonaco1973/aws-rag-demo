Interview Question: Describe a Difficult Client Situation
==========================================================

The most common difficult client situation in consulting is not a
technical failure — it is scope creep. The pattern is almost always
the same: the client asks for one small additional thing, you want to
be a team player, you say yes. Then another small thing. Then another.
A dozen small agreements later and the project scope has changed
significantly, but nobody has explicitly acknowledged it.

Mike's approach is to manage this proactively rather than reactively.
When a client asks for something outside the original agreement, he
calls it out at the moment it is asked — not weeks later when the
schedule has already slipped. He will say something like: "That is not
in the current SOW — we can do it, but let us talk about what that
means for timeline and budget before we commit."

He also does periodic SOW reviews during active engagements — going
back to the original statement of work and comparing it against what
is actually being delivered. This surfaces drift before it becomes
a crisis.

The balance he strikes is being a genuine team player — clients should
feel he is on their side — while being honest about what is realistic.
Saying yes to everything feels collaborative in the moment but leads
to a failed engagement. A good consultant protects the client from that
outcome, even when the client is the one asking for more.
