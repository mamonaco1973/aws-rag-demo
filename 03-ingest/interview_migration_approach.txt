Interview Question: How Do You Approach a New Cloud Migration Engagement?
=========================================================================

Every engagement starts with a discovery period. The goal is to deeply
understand the client's domain, existing technology stack, and what
success actually looks like for their stakeholders. This involves
structured conversations with technical and business stakeholders, and
sometimes researching technology specific to the client's environment.

During discovery, Mike favors building MVPs — minimum viable products
that give stakeholders hands-on experience with a reasonable
representation of the final solution. This surfaces misaligned
expectations early, when they are cheap to fix, rather than late when
they are expensive.

Discovery ends with a formal architecture document and validated
requirements. In regulated pharmaceutical environments, this also
includes settling on a validation strategy — drafting IQ (Installation
Qualification), OQ (Operational Qualification), and PQ (Performance
Qualification) documents before any production work begins.

Execution follows a structured sequence: validation environment first,
then production, then DR. Each phase is deliberate.

At the end of the engagement, Mike prioritizes a formal support handoff
before go-live. Consultants remain available during a hypercare period —
a window where the delivery team is directly involved in support
alongside the client's operations team. The length of hypercare depends
on how well the client's support team is trained, which is directly
tied to documentation quality. Better documentation means a shorter
hypercare window and a cleaner exit.
